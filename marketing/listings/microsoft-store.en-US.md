## Description
Gryt is a place a community sits in. The channels and roles stay put, and people drop in when they feel like it.

You run the server. Point the app at a machine you control. A spare PC in the corner counts. That's where the messages live, and there's no company holding them and no trial tier that expires.

Voice goes over WebRTC through an SFU, so one server relays the audio and a call with eight people in it doesn't turn into eight separate uploads from your laptop. Screen sharing takes the audio with it, and video works the same way.

Messages are encrypted and the keys are yours. Your identity is a keypair the app makes and keeps on your device, not an account somebody else can lock you out of.

The whole thing is open source under the AGPL: the desktop client, the server, the media server, the docs, and a written policy on how much of it was built with AI and how you can check that yourself with git log.

Features
- Text channels, roles and permissions that stay put
- Group voice calls over WebRTC, with an SFU rather than mesh
- Video and screen sharing, screen audio included
- Direct messages
- Encrypted messages, with keys held on your own devices
- Servers you host, found on your network or reached over the internet
- Push-to-talk and hotkeys that work outside the app
- A tray icon that shows whether you're muted, deafened or live

Scope
Gryt is for a friend group on a spare PC, and it isn't trying to be compliant with anything. There's no enterprise tier and no support contract. If you need either, this is the wrong tool and that's fine.

Getting a server
The docs at docs.gryt.chat walk through running one. There's a Docker image, and a CLI if you'd rather not write compose files.
